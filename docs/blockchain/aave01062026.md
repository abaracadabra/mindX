# Aave Security Incidents (March–June 2026) and a Scenario-Based AAVE Price Outlook

## Key Findings

**1. Clarifying the "$270M ETH / bridge" claim.** There was no single ~$270M exploit of Aave's own contracts. The closest matches in this window:
- **Drift Protocol (Solana), ~$270–286M, April 1, 2026** — a months-long social-engineering/admin-key compromise (North Korea, UNC4736). Unrelated to Aave. CoinDesk reported "$270 million"; Elliptic calculated $286M; other coverage cited $285M. The variance reflects different on-chain tallies and token prices.
- **KelpDAO rsETH bridge exploit, ~$292M, April 18, 2026** — the event that hit Aave. An attacker forged a LayerZero cross-chain message (1-of-1 DVN, RPC-node poisoning) to mint 116,500 unbacked rsETH, deposited ~89,567 as Aave collateral and borrowed ~$190M+ WETH/wstETH, exposing Aave to bad debt modeled up to ~$230M.

**2. Three additional Aave-ecosystem incidents in the window:**
- **CAPO oracle misconfiguration (March 10, 2026):** ~$27M in wrongful wstETH E-Mode liquidations; no bad debt; users fully reimbursed (~345 ETH).
- **HypurrFi/Aave V3 rounding-bug disclosure (March 5–6, 2026):** a latent rounding flaw in Aave V3 pre-3.5; an Aave fork (dTRINITY) was later exploited for ~$257K via the same vector.
- **GHO stablecoin stress:** GHO's Stability Module was impaired during the rsETH crisis, prompting Curve to remove GHO from crvUSD pricing; the peg has since held ~$1.

**3. Recovery was a landmark of DeFi self-organization** — no socialized losses, Umbrella not slashed, coalition over-funded the gap.

**4. Fundamentals weakened sharply but are stabilizing:** TVL fell from a $30.25B Nov-2025 peak (and ~$26.4B pre-exploit) to ~$14.5B; revenue/fees down ~60%+ post-exploit; but V4 is gaining and the buyback/Aavenomics machinery is intact.

**Macro context:** Per DefiLlama (via BeInCrypto/Yahoo Finance), April 2026 was crypto's worst month for hacks on record — **$606.2 million stolen across 12 incidents, 3.7x Q1's $165.5M**, with Drift (~$285M) and KelpDAO (~$292M) alone accounting for ~95% of losses. TRM Labs attributed 76% of all 2026 crypto-hack losses through April to DPRK-linked groups.

## Details

### The incidents, in order

**A) CAPO Oracle Misconfiguration — March 10, 2026 (~$27M wrongful liquidations)**
A misconfiguration in Aave's Correlated Asset Price Oracle (CAPO) caused Aave V3 Ethereum Core and Prime to value wstETH ~2.85% below the live market rate (≈1.1939 vs ≈1.228 wstETH-per-ETH). This pushed 34 high-leverage E-Mode positions below liquidation thresholds, liquidating ~10,938 wstETH (~$27M). Root cause (per Chaos Labs' post-mortem): a mismatch between the on-chain snapshot ratio (~1.15, set in 2024) and snapshot timestamp under a 3%/3-day update constraint, which rejected the correct update and left a stale cap. Liquidator bots captured ~499 ETH (~$1.2M) in bonuses. Aave incurred **no bad debt**; it recovered 141 ETH via BuilderNet refunds plus 13 ETH in fees, and the DAO treasury covered the remaining shortfall to fully reimburse the ~345 ETH (~$700K) excess-liquidation loss. Fixed the same day via the Risk Steward; borrow caps temporarily cut to 1. **Status: resolved.** Notable: it was the first operational CAPO failure after 1,200+ payloads/3,000+ parameters over a year, and community members questioned whether risk firm Chaos Labs shares liability. It also coincided with governance turmoil (ACI's announced exit on March 3).

**B) HypurrFi disclosure of an Aave V3 rounding bug — March 5–6, 2026**
HypurrFi (a HyperEVM Aave-fork lending market) disclosed a rounding vulnerability in Aave V3 versions prior to 3.5 allowing extraction of underlying tokens via repeated supply/withdraw and borrow/repay cycles. HypurrFi paused its XAUT0 and UBTC markets; no user funds were lost there. The timing was awkward — days after Aave Labs published a V4 security report (345 review-days; Certora, ChainSecurity, Trail of Bits, Blackthorn; 900+ Sherlock researchers; "no critical or high-severity vulnerabilities"). On March 17, the Aave fork **dTRINITY (dLEND) was exploited for ~$257K** via the same class of cbBTC aToken share-math rounding flaw. **Status: contained at Aave core; fork ecosystem partially affected.**

**C) KelpDAO rsETH LayerZero bridge exploit — April 18, 2026 (~$292M; the central event)**
At 17:35 UTC (block 24,908,285), an attacker exploited Kelp's LayerZero V2 Unichain→Ethereum rsETH route, configured as a 1-of-1 DVN. By poisoning internal RPC nodes (and DDoS-ing external ones), the attacker fed a false "burn" to the single verifier, which approved a forged message releasing 116,500 rsETH (~$292M) on Ethereum with no backing. Rather than dumping, the attacker deposited ~89,567 rsETH across 8 Aave V3 positions (Ethereum Core + Arbitrum) and borrowed ~82,650 WETH ($190.86M) + 821 wstETH. The single largest position supplied 53,000 rsETH and borrowed 52,460 WETH (~$121M). Because rsETH became unbacked, those positions were not normally liquidatable; Marc Zeller publicly urged WETH suppliers to withdraw immediately.

*Attribution:* Per Chainalysis, "LayerZero has attributed the operation to the Democratic People's Republic of Korea (DPRK)'s Lazarus Group, and specifically the sub-group known as TraderTraitor." LayerZero said it would no longer sign messages for 1-of-1 DVN apps (~40% of LayerZero apps then ran 1-of-1). Kelp migrated toward Chainlink CCIP.

*Impact:* Aave's smart contracts were **not** compromised, but contagion was severe. Aave's incident report modeled bad debt of **$123.7M (if losses spread across all rsETH) to $230.1M (if isolated to L2s)**; some coverage cited "$177 million in bad debt that now sits on Aave's books" (KuCoin/crypto.news) against the ~$190M WETH borrowed. Aave TVL collapsed (~$26.4B → ~$14.5B; supplied assets fell from ~$41–44B), and DeFi-wide TVL dropped ~$13B. The April 20 report disclosed the DAO treasury at ~$181M ($62M ETH-correlated, $54M AAVE, $52M stablecoins) with 2025 revenue of $145M and operating cash flow of $149M.

*Recovery (the standout achievement):*
- **"DeFi United"** coalition formed (Aave, Lido, EtherFi, Mantle, Consensys/Joseph Lubin, Ethena, LayerZero, Compound, Stani Kulechov personally, others). Per AMBCrypto, the coalition "exceeded the $230 million shortfall by 39%, raising the total to $320 million." Consensys/Joseph Lubin committed up to 30,000 ETH; Mantle 30,000 ETH; Aave DAO 25,000 ETH; Kulechov 5,000 ETH personally.
- Recovery used **temporary oracle adjustments to liquidate the 8 attacker positions** (AIP 478, May 6) and refill the rsETH lockbox in tranches — explicitly designed to **avoid socializing losses** to rsETH holders, a deliberate break from past DeFi failures.
- Per CoinDesk (May 18): "About 112,103 unbacked rsETH was created in the exploit, with roughly 106,993 since recovered… 89,567 recovered through Aave liquidations and another 17,426 recovered through Compound, leaving a residual shortfall of about 5,200 rsETH," which DeFi United covered.
- WETH LTVs were restored across six V3 networks (Ethereum Core, Prime, Arbitrum, Base, Mantle, Linea) on May 18; the rsETH lockbox was refilled over five tranches (May 13–26, ~116,131 rsETH); Aave declared "rsETH and all Aave markets are operating normally" on May 25.
- **The Umbrella safety module was NOT slashed.** Early warnings (April 18–20) that aWETH stakers and WETH depositors faced automatic slashing/haircuts were not borne out; the coalition funds plus liquidation recovery covered the gap, so there was **no WETH-depositor haircut and no socialized loss.** This was the first real-world test of the Umbrella system (which replaced the legacy Safety Module in late 2025) — and it ended without an actual slash.
- Aave overhauled listing standards (adding cybersecurity, interoperability, architecture, and bridge/oracle/custodian review), executed ~295 parameter changes (168 supply-cap and 66 borrow-cap reductions), and proposed automated defenses that drop an asset's LTV to zero when risk thresholds breach.

*Unresolved legal thread:* Arbitrum's Security Council froze 30,765.67 ETH (~$71M) tied to the attacker. Families holding ~$877M in unpaid North Korea terrorism judgments (Gerstein Harrow LLP) obtained an SDNY restraining notice (May 1). Aave moved to vacate it (May 4), with Kulechov arguing "a thief does not own what he steals." Judge Margaret Garnett modified the order (May 8–9) to allow an Arbitrum DAO transfer to an Aave LLC wallet while preserving the creditors' claims; Arbitrum delegates approved release with ~91% support (182.2M ARB). The judge then **paused** a final decision, ordered supplemental briefs (May 22), and scheduled a **June 5, 2026 hearing** — whose outcome was not yet reported as of June 1. **Status of the broader incident: substantially resolved; legal ownership of the $71M frozen ETH still pending.**

### Fundamentals (mid-2026)
- **Price/market cap:** Per CoinGecko (June 1, 2026), AAVE was $82.54 with a $1,255,069,462 market cap (rank #64), "trading −87.50% below" the $661.69 ATH (set May 18, 2021); −22.7% over 30 days and ~−60% over 12 months. Circulating supply ~15.18M of a 16M hard cap (≈95%), with no team vesting cliffs remaining.
- **TVL:** ~$14.5B combined (DefiLlama, May 18), down ~52% from the $30.25B November-2025 peak; V3 ≈96.6% of TVL across 21 chains; ~74.8% utilization; Ethereum anchors ~$11.5B.
- **Revenue:** 2025 revenue ~$140–145M (gross fees ~$1B); DefiLlama annualized "earnings" ~$80–100M; post-exploit weekly fees/revenue fell ~66%/62% as borrow demand evaporated.
- **V4:** Mainnet launched March 30, 2026 (modular hub-and-spoke). V4 TVL grew ~150% to a ~$105M peak before settling ~$75M by late May — still small but resilient through the crisis.
- **GHO:** ~$580–584M market cap, peg holding ~$1; Savings GHO (sGHO) ~4.25–5.6% APY; 100% of GHO borrow interest flows to the DAO; GHO contributes ~10% of protocol revenue. The core GHO growth team is reportedly slated to exit July 2026 — a watch item.
- **Aavenomics/buyback:** A permanent **$50M/year buyback** ("Buy & Distribute," weekly tranches $250K–$1.75M); a May–Nov 2025 pilot retired 94,000+ AAVE (~$22M). The "Aave Will Win" framework routes 100% of product revenue (core protocol, Aave Pro/V4, the App, Horizon RWA) to the DAO treasury. Umbrella replaced the legacy Safety Module.
- **Governance friction:** BGD Labs (which built V3) exited April 1, 2026; ACI/Marc Zeller wound down its mandate — both citing tensions with Aave Labs over the V3→V4 migration and the "Aave Will Win" funding (Zeller called it evidence "a single entity holds enough voting power to pass its own budget proposals"). A meaningful talent loss.
- **Competition:** Aave remains #1 in lending (~$14.5–19.4B TVL across 21+ chains, ~40–45% share). Morpho surged to ~$7–11.8B TVL on institutional distribution (Coinbase USDC loans, Apollo, EF deposits), and its isolated-market design contained rsETH contagion to ~$1M — a competitive-narrative win that institutional capital noticed. Spark ~$3–6.8B; Compound ~$1.2–2.7B.
